<section class="faq-category" id="about">
  <header><span>01</span><div><p>About PPBS</p><h2>History, purpose, and influences</h2></div></header>
  <div class="faq-category__body">
    <details open><summary>What is PPBS?</summary><p>The Psychology of Political Behavior Studies is a cumulative political-psychology research programme and research infrastructure. It brings political attitudes, identities, knowledge, participation, and voting into the same studies as validated psychological measures. Its design supports theory comparison, independent-sample confirmation, longitudinal questions where recontacts exist, measurement research, and reproducible reuse. PPBS is a programme rather than one survey or one permanently fixed dataset.</p></details>
    <details><summary>What is the history of PPBS? How did it come to be?</summary><p>The documented public core begins with the 2016 United States election cycle and continues through the 2018 and 2020 cycles. It combined national quota cross-sections with independent confirmatory samples and, for some questions, recontacts of earlier participants. The original need was practical and scientific: political psychology often studies nearby explanations in separate datasets, making direct comparison and independent confirmation difficult. PPBS brought many of those constructs into a cumulative design. Over time, the work expanded from collecting studies to building an infrastructure of codebooks, harmonisation decisions, reports, reproducible workflows, proposal-based collaboration, and mentorship. Later and international collections are being developed, but their exact public scope is verified separately before PPBS reports totals.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Why develop a mentored research programme?</summary><p>Research opportunity depends on more than a strong idea. Data access, protected time, specialist methods support, reliable computing, mentorship, language support, and publication networks are distributed unevenly. These barriers are especially consequential for scholars in low- and middle-income countries and researchers from minoritized or underrepresented groups. The programme creates a supported path through the parts PPBS can influence directly: low-burden entry, substantive feedback, bounded access, sustained mentoring, public contribution credit, and accountable decisions.</p></details>
  </div>

<section class="faq-category" id="research-possibilities">
  <header><span>02</span><div><p>Research possibilities</p><h2>Questions the infrastructure can help answer</h2></div></header>
  <div class="faq-category__body">
    <details open><summary>What kinds of projects could the programme support?</summary><p>Projects may reproduce an existing analysis, test robustness across defensible analytic choices, replicate a finding in an independent sample, examine whether a pattern generalises across surveys or contexts, compare theories measured in the same data, analyse political change across waves, or improve the measurement of a political-psychology construct. A project may also build reusable infrastructure: construct crosswalks, codebooks, harmonised variables, question banks, data diagnostics, or accessible methods resources.</p></details>
    <details><summary>How does PPBS evaluate the strength of a claim?</summary><p>PPBS builds a layered evidence profile. It asks whether the claim can be reconstructed from its items and decisions; reproduced computationally; stress-tested across defensible analyses; confirmed in an independent sample; traced across election cycles and, separately, within people when recontacts exist; and generalised across contexts only after design and measurement differences have been checked. A versioned claim-level record preserves the estimand, provenance, code, review decisions, limitations, and unresolved questions. <a href="methodology.html">Read the full framework</a>.</p></details>
    <details><summary>How could a project reproduce, replicate, or extend a published paper?</summary><p>A team can begin by reconstructing the original sample, variables, scoring, exclusions, and models. It can then examine defensible alternatives, move to a held-out or independently collected PPBS sample, and finally assess comparable evidence in another wave, country, culture, or survey infrastructure. Original authors may be invited to clarify decisions and context while the verification team retains independent control of its analysis and conclusions. A difference across contexts can reveal a boundary condition rather than a simple failure.</p></details>
    <details><summary>What psychometric projects are possible?</summary><p>PPBS contains many multi-item measures used across samples and periods. Projects can assess dimensionality, reliability, convergent and discriminant validity, item performance, response styles, measurement invariance across groups, countries or time, and differential item functioning. Suitable methods may include confirmatory factor analysis, item response theory, network psychometrics, and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0174035">exploratory graph analysis</a>. Longitudinal comparisons need evidence that repeated measures retain a comparable meaning; <a href="https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000075">Liu, Millsap, West, Tein, Tanaka, and Grimm (2017)</a> illustrate why that assumption matters for ordered-categorical measures.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Can a project be exploratory?</summary><p>Yes. Exploratory work can discover patterns, refine measures, expose boundary conditions, and generate sharper predictions. PPBS can then ask whether a pattern recurs in an independent sample from the same period, across waves, across countries or cultures, or longitudinally when recontact data are available. The research record identifies which decisions were exploratory, which were made before access, and which evidence arrived later.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Must a project use one preferred statistical method?</summary><p>The question, design, estimand, and measurement determine the methods. Many projects benefit from more than one model, operationalisation, sample, or design because each exposes a different vulnerability. Frequentist, Bayesian, estimation, design-based, qualitative, computational, mixed-method, and other approaches are welcome when they strengthen the evidence and their assumptions are visible.</p></details>

<section class="faq-category" id="data-access">
  <header><span>03</span><div><p>Data & access</p><h2>Different data require different doors</h2></div></header>
  <div class="faq-category__body">
    <details open><summary>Do all research infrastructures provide data in the same way?</summary><p>No. Access ranges from immediate public downloads to registration, institutional licences, controlled environments, and proposal-based collaboration. Roper’s <a href="https://ropercenter.cornell.edu/ipoll">iPoll</a>, for example, provides fuller access through member institutions, while NZAVS uses controlled collaboration and data-sharing procedures. PPBS states the conditions for each approved project rather than describing all data as uniformly open or closed.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Does selection provide access to all PPBS data?</summary><p>Access is bounded, staged, and project-specific. It depends on data availability, consent, ethics, confidentiality, institutional requirements, the approved plan, and team capacity. A project may start with one dataset or variable set and add an independent replication sample, another wave, a country comparison, or longitudinal recontacts after the team has justified that next step.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Why is a confidentiality or non-disclosure agreement needed?</summary><p>Participant-level data are embargoed and released through controlled access. The agreement defines secure handling, permitted uses, sharing, retention, and publication. PPBS is simultaneously building public infrastructure around protected data: codebooks, item wording, sample and wave metadata, coding and harmonisation decisions, registered plans, amendments, and analysis materials where responsible. Public documentation enables scrutiny and correction without disclosing protected participant data.</p></details>
    <details><summary>What can be registered when the data already exist?</summary><p>A researcher without prior access can register the question, hypotheses, samples, variables, scoring, exclusions, models, contingencies, diagnostics, and interpretation rules before access. Researchers with previous exposure declare what they have seen and register the consequential choices that remain. Held-out or independently collected samples can preserve a stronger confirmatory test.</p></details>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="faq-category" id="programme">
  <header><span>04</span><div><p>Mentorship</p><h2>Leadership with a support structure</h2></div></header>
  <div class="faq-category__body">
    <details open><summary>Who is the programme designed to support?</summary><p>The pilot is intended primarily for doctoral researchers and scholars in the early years after the PhD, with flexibility for career interruptions and non-linear paths. Priority is planned for applicants in low- and middle-income countries, researchers from underrepresented contexts, and scholars whose institutions provide limited access to large datasets, methods support, or international publication networks. Final eligibility rules will be published before applications open.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Does selection guarantee a publication?</summary><p>Selection begins a serious, supported effort to produce and share a rigorous output. Health, care, funding, job changes, migration, leaving academia, team capacity, and publication processes can affect whether and when a paper appears. PPBS continues the research process across supported, unsupported, uncertain, and practically negligible findings. This results-agnostic commitment draws on <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459211007467">evidence about publication bias and Registered Reports</a> and the <a href="https://doi.org/10.1027/1864-9335/a000192">original Registered Reports proposal</a>.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Must a project finish within a fixed period?</summary><p>Teams agree a realistic trajectory and regular review points, then revise them as circumstances change. Project complexity, care, health, safety, employment, language, infrastructure, and unequal access to protected time all matter. Expectations remain explicit while pauses, extensions, and non-linear progress are handled with context and humane accountability.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Are applications open?</summary><p>Not yet. PPBS will publish the final protocol, eligibility rules, review rubric, mentor capacity, timetable, application form, data-access conditions, and named accountability contacts before opening the pilot.</p></details>

<section class="faq-category" id="accountability">
  <header><span>05</span><div><p>Credit & accountability</p><h2>Rules that remain visible when decisions become difficult</h2></div></header>
  <div class="faq-category__body">
    <details open><summary>Will every meaningful contribution be visible?</summary><p>PPBS logs contributions using <a href="https://tenzing.club/">Tenzing</a> and the <a href="https://credit.niso.org/">CRediT taxonomy</a>, archives the record, and, with consent, connects people, projects, and roles in a public register inspired by <a href="https://forrt.org/contributors/">FORRT’s contributor pages</a>. Authorship remains a separate decision tied to the work on a specific output and the responsibilities each author explicitly accepts.</p></details>
    <details><summary>How does social responsibility enter a replication or verification project?</summary><p>Methodological soundness is one part of rigour. Adapted from the <a href="https://www.replicationresearch.org/constitution/">R² constitution</a>, PPBS asks projects to consider generalisability, positionality, power, reflexivity, foreseeable harm, and historical context. Any participant can request an independent responsibility review. The team records the concern, response, safeguards, and route for appeal. Original-author engagement can improve contextual accuracy, while independent reviewers preserve the verification team’s autonomy.</p></details>
    <details><summary>How are conflicts of interest handled?</summary><p>Applicants, mentors, reviewers, data stewards, and decision-makers declare relevant financial and non-financial interests and update them when circumstances change. These may include funding, employment or supervision, close relationships, rivalry, competition, advocacy or political ties, prior public commitments, or strong intellectual investment in a claim. Management can include disclosure, recusal, an additional independent reviewer, or independent adjudication.</p></details>
    <details><summary>How can a participant raise a concern?</summary><p>The pilot will name a confidential adviser outside the participant’s immediate mentoring and decision chain. A participant may seek guidance, correction, mediation, recusal, or independent review. The protocol will state who receives concerns, the limits of confidentiality, response times, appeal routes, recordkeeping, and protection against retaliation. Raising a concern in good faith must not reduce access, credit, authorship consideration, or future opportunity.</p></details>
    <details><summary>What remains to be finalised before the pilot opens?</summary><p>The local programme design is a public draft. PPBS still needs to name the confidential adviser and independent responsibility reviewer, confirm anti-retaliation and safeguarding language, approve the conflict-of-interest declaration, specify appeal and response timelines, confirm mentor capacity, and approve the final data-use and confidentiality agreements. These are implementation decisions rather than promises the website should imply are already operational.</p></details>
