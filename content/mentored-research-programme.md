<section class="mentorship-intro" aria-labelledby="mentorship-intro-title">
  <div>
    <p class="eyebrow">Research access · sustained mentorship · applicant leadership</p>
    <h2 id="mentorship-intro-title">A good question deserves more than permission to enter.</h2>
    <p class="mentorship-lede">The PPBS Mentored Research Programme is being developed for early-career researchers who want to lead rigorous, reproducible work with Psychology of Political Behavior Studies data. Selected researchers receive sustained theoretical, methodological, reproducibility, writing, and data-specific support while retaining intellectual leadership of their projects.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="principle-card principle-card--door">
    <p class="principle-card__number" aria-hidden="true">01</p>
    <p><strong>Opening the door is the beginning</strong></p>
    <p>Talent is global. Access to data, protected time, methods support, mentorship, and publication networks is distributed unevenly. PPBS can act on the parts it controls: accessible entry, useful feedback, sustained support, transparent decisions, public credit, and accountable governance.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="purpose">
  <div class="section-kicker">01 · Purpose</div>
  <h2>What is the programme for?</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">The programme helps researchers who have a consequential political-psychology question but may not yet have the data access, specialist support, international network, or protected research time needed to pursue it well. It turns access into a developmental partnership: sharpen the question, verify what PPBS can actually measure, register the decisions that can still be made prospectively, build a reproducible workflow, and communicate the findings with appropriate restraint.</p>
  <div class="purpose-principles" aria-label="What the programme promises">
    <article><strong>Lead your question</strong><p>The selected researcher ordinarily remains the intellectual lead and intended first author.</p></article>
    <article><strong>Receive useful review</strong><p>Every eligible proposal receives a decision, concise reasons, and structured developmental feedback.</p></article>
    <article><strong>Use the methods the question needs</strong><p>No inferential school receives automatic priority. Choices follow the question, design, estimand, and data.</p></article>
    <article><strong>Follow a results-agnostic pathway</strong><p>Review focuses on rigor, transparency, interpretation, and learning across supported, unsupported, uncertain, and practically negligible findings.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section project-studio" id="project-studio">
  <div class="section-kicker">02 · Project studio</div>
  <h2>Many questions can enter through this door</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">A PPBS project can begin with a published claim, a measurement problem, an unexplained difference between places or periods, or a pattern that deserves a stronger test. The programme adapts the layered verification logic being developed in ARISE: reproduce the result, test reasonable alternatives, examine an independent sample, and then ask where the pattern travels.</p>
  <div class="project-ladder" aria-label="Six families of projects supported by the programme">
    <article><span>01</span><h3>Reproduce</h3><p>Rebuild a published or internal result from its data, code, construct definitions, and sample decisions. Discrepancies become documented learning rather than a contest.</p></article>
    <article><span>02</span><h3>Stress-test</h3><p>Map how conclusions respond to defensible choices in exclusions, scoring, covariates, estimands, models, missing-data handling, or weighting.</p></article>
    <article><span>03</span><h3>Replicate</h3><p>Test a claim in an independent PPBS sample, a later wave, or another large survey whose measures support a defensible comparison.</p></article>
    <article><span>04</span><h3>Compare contexts</h3><p>Examine commonalities and differences across countries, cultures, political periods, survey infrastructures, or population groups. Contextual variation is a finding to explain.</p></article>
    <article><span>05</span><h3>Improve measurement</h3><p>Study dimensionality, reliability, validity, measurement invariance, differential item functioning, response styles, item response theory, or exploratory graph analysis for widely used political-psychology scales.</p></article>
    <article><span>06</span><h3>Trace change</h3><p>Use repeated cross-sections or longitudinal recontacts to distinguish historical shifts, population differences, and within-person change where the design permits it.</p></article>
  </div>
  <div class="project-studio__footer">
    <p><strong>Across the ladder:</strong> PPBS can support construct harmonisation, survey-question mapping, codebook and metadata work, accessible research summaries, and methods resources that make later projects easier to verify.</p>
    <a href="faq.html#research-possibilities">Explore datasets and project examples <span aria-hidden="true">→</span></a>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="pathway">
  <div class="section-kicker">03 · The pathway</div>
  <h2>Two gates, followed by a completion review</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">There are <strong>two developmental gates</strong>. Gate A selects a small cohort for proposal development. Gate B approves the registered analysis plan and project-specific data access. The later completion review is a quality checkpoint, not a third selection gate.</p>
  <div class="gate-key" aria-label="Pathway key">
    <span><i class="gate-swatch" aria-hidden="true"></i><strong>Gate A</strong> cohort decision</span>
    <span><i class="gate-swatch" aria-hidden="true"></i><strong>Gate B</strong> plan and access approval</span>
    <span><i class="review-swatch" aria-hidden="true"></i><strong>Completion review</strong> research-quality checkpoint</span>
  </div>
  <figure class="programme-flow" aria-labelledby="programme-flow-title">
    <figcaption id="programme-flow-title">Proposed PPBS review and mentorship process</figcaption>
    <ol>
      <li><span class="flow-number">1</span><strong>Short idea</strong><small>Question, contribution, PPBS fit, prior data exposure, learning goals</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">2</span><strong>Eligibility & data fit</strong><small>Measures, samples, governance, duplication, feasibility</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">3</span><strong>Structured review</strong><small>Calibrated rubric, narrative feedback, conflicts managed</small></li>
      <li class="flow-gate"><span class="flow-number">A</span><strong>Cohort decision</strong><small>Scientific promise, developmental value, equity, capacity</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">4</span><strong>Proposal development</strong><small>Mentoring team, workshops, peer cohort, revision</small></li>
      <li class="flow-gate"><span class="flow-number">B</span><strong>Plan & access approval</strong><small>Registered analysis plan and project-specific access</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">5</span><strong>Research cycle</strong><small>Reproducible analysis, transparent amendments, writing</small></li>
      <li class="flow-completion"><span class="flow-number">✓</span><strong>Completion review</strong><small>Substance, measurement, analysis, reproducibility, reporting</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">6</span><strong>Durable outputs</strong><small>Registration, code, manuscript or report, accessible summary</small></li>
    </ol>
  </figure>
  <div class="process-notes">
    <div><strong>Gate A is developmental.</strong><p>It selects a feasible cohort that PPBS can support well. Reviewers should not reward fashionable topics, famous institutions, or overconfident claims.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Gate B protects the research.</strong><p>Researchers register the decisions that can still be made prospectively and complete the required onboarding before outcome-rich data are released. Existing exposure is declared, not hidden.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Completion is results-agnostic.</strong><p>A well-conducted project remains valuable when estimates are uncertain, hypotheses are unsupported, or effects are practically negligible.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="eligibility">
  <div class="section-kicker">04 · Applicants</div>
  <h2>Who the programme is designed to support</h2>
  <p>PPBS intends to prioritize early-career researchers—including doctoral researchers and scholars in the early years after the PhD—with flexibility for career interruptions and non-linear career paths. Particular priority will be given to applicants in low- and middle-income countries, researchers from underrepresented contexts, and scholars whose institutions provide limited access to large datasets, specialist methods support, or international publication networks.</p>
  <div class="eligibility-grid">
    <div><h3>A strong application needs</h3><ul><li>A clear and consequential research question</li><li>A plausible connection to PPBS constructs, samples, or design</li><li>An honest account of prior access to the data or results</li><li>A realistic initial scope</li><li>Specific learning goals and support needs</li><li>Commitment to transparent and reproducible research</li></ul></div>
    <div><h3>You can apply without</h3><ul><li>A fully written manuscript at the first stage</li><li>Advanced programming skills before the programme begins</li><li>A prestigious institution or established international network</li><li>A promise of a positive, significant, or novel result</li><li>Commitment to one statistical or inferential tradition</li><li>Prior publication in an English-language journal</li></ul></div>
  </div>
  <aside class="onboarding-note" aria-label="Data access onboarding note">
    <h3>Data access comes after onboarding</h3>
    <p>Selection establishes eligibility for the supported pathway. Data are released in bounded, project-specific stages after approved researchers complete confidentiality or non-disclosure and data-use commitments, secure-handling requirements, and any applicable ethics or institutional documentation. The final agreement specifies what can be accessed, stored, shared, and published.</p>
  </aside>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="application">
  <div class="section-kicker">05 · Application</div>
  <h2>A low-burden first stage</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">The first application should be short enough that researchers without extensive grant-writing support can participate, while still giving PPBS enough information to assess fit and mentoring needs.</p>
  <div class="application-grid">
    <article><span>01</span><h3>Research question</h3><p>State the main question in plain language and explain why answering it would matter.</p></article>
    <article><span>02</span><h3>PPBS fit</h3><p>Name the constructs, comparison, sample type, wave, or design feature you believe is relevant. Exact variables can be refined later.</p></article>
    <article><span>03</span><h3>Contribution</h3><p>Explain whether the project tests, compares, replicates, extends, or challenges an existing claim.</p></article>
    <article><span>04</span><h3>Prior knowledge</h3><p>Disclose any previous access to PPBS data, reports, code, descriptive statistics, or closely related results.</p></article>
    <article><span>05</span><h3>Learning goals</h3><p>Describe the theoretical, methodological, computational, writing, or career support that would make the project developmental.</p></article>
    <article><span>06</span><h3>Practical scope</h3><p>Provide the expected time commitment, relevant skills, constraints, collaborators, and a realistic working rhythm.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="review">
  <div class="section-kicker">06 · Review</div>
  <h2>A review process designed for fairness and useful feedback</h2>
  <div class="review-layout">
    <div>
      <h3>Eligibility and feasibility screen</h3>
      <p>PPBS first checks whether the question can be addressed with the documented data, whether the project conflicts with an active or reserved analysis, and whether the proposed use is consistent with consent, privacy, and access conditions. Scientific and developmental review begins after this eligibility screen.</p>
      <h3>Structured scientific review</h3>
      <p>Eligible ideas are reviewed against the same published criteria. Review combines scores with concise reasons, uncertainty, and suggestions. Scores are calibrated before use and should not be treated as mechanically precise.</p>
      <h3>Cohort construction</h3>
      <p>The final decision considers proposal quality alongside developmental benefit, representation, portfolio balance, mentor fit, and the number of projects PPBS can responsibly support. These considerations are reported transparently rather than hidden inside a single score.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="rubric-card">
      <h3>Proposed review dimensions</h3>
      <ol>
        <li><strong>Importance:</strong> Is the question worth answering?</li>
        <li><strong>Data fit:</strong> Can PPBS measure what the proposal claims?</li>
        <li><strong>Inferential clarity:</strong> Are the intended quantities and claims coherent?</li>
        <li><strong>Feasibility:</strong> Can the work proceed with available data and support?</li>
        <li><strong>Transparency:</strong> Can key decisions be specified and audited?</li>
        <li><strong>Developmental value:</strong> Would mentorship materially expand the applicant’s capacity or access?</li>
        <li><strong>Responsible use:</strong> Are limitations, participant protections, and contextual risks taken seriously?</li>
      </ol>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="safeguard-strip">
    <span>Anonymous review where feasible</span><span>Declared conflicts of interest</span><span>At least two perspectives per shortlisted idea</span><span>Independent adjudication of close cases</span><span>Structured feedback to every eligible applicant</span><span>Correction and complaints route</span>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="mentoring">
  <div class="section-kicker">07 · Mentoring</div>
  <h2>A team around each lead researcher</h2>
  <div class="mentor-team" aria-label="Mentoring team roles">
    <article><span>Lead researcher</span><h3>Leads the project</h3><p>Owns the question, directs the work, makes documented decisions, leads the analysis and writing, and communicates support needs.</p></article>
    <article><span>Substantive mentor</span><h3>Strengthens the argument</h3><p>Challenges the contribution, theory, construct interpretation, contextual assumptions, and connection to the literature.</p></article>
    <article><span>Methods & reproducibility mentor</span><h3>Strengthens the evidence</h3><p>Supports design, analysis planning, sensitivity checks, code review, workflow design, and transparent inference.</p></article>
    <article><span>PPBS data steward</span><h3>Protects data meaning</h3><p>Verifies variable provenance, sample definitions, harmonization, permissible access, and limitations of the available data.</p></article>
    <article class="programme-steward"><span>Founding PI & programme steward</span><h3>Safeguards continuity</h3><p>Connects each project to the programme built over more than a decade, convenes cross-role decisions, safeguards PPBS governance, and supports the team without displacing the lead researcher.</p></article>
  </div>
  <p>The programme will combine project meetings, cohort workshops, peer feedback, and asynchronous review. Each team will begin with a mentoring compact covering goals, roles, communication, response times, accessibility needs, authorship expectations, disagreement procedures, and boundaries. More than one named contact will be available if problems arise.</p>
  <p><strong>Mentoring is scholarly work.</strong> Sustained mentorship that substantively shapes the question, theory, design, analysis, interpretation, or writing may support authorship when the full authorship criteria are met. Occasional advice, administrative oversight, status, or access alone does not. The same contribution-based standard applies to every role, including the PI.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="contribution-credit">
  <div class="section-kicker">08 · Contribution, credit, and authorship</div>
  <h2>Every meaningful contribution has a record</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">Contribution is the work people do. Credit is how PPBS makes that work visible. Authorship is one form of credit for a particular scholarly output, with additional responsibilities. Each decision serves a distinct purpose.</p>
  <div class="credit-path" aria-label="From contribution to authorship">
    <article><span>1</span><div><strong>Log the work</strong><p>Meaningful contributions are recorded prospectively and updated as the project changes.</p></div></article>
    <article><span>2</span><div><strong>Preserve the record</strong><p>The contribution log is archived and, with contributors’ consent, reflected on the PPBS Contributors page even when no paper follows.</p></div></article>
    <article><span>3</span><div><strong>Match credit to contribution</strong><p>Recognition may include authorship, named contributorship, group credit, acknowledgement, citation, mentoring credit, or compensation.</p></div></article>
    <article><span>4</span><div><strong>Confirm authorship</strong><p>When work becomes a paper, authorship follows actual scholarly contributions, critical engagement with the manuscript, final approval, and accountability.</p></div></article>
  </div>
  <p>PPBS uses the <a href="https://credit.niso.org/">CRediT Contributor Role Taxonomy</a> as a shared vocabulary for describing who did what. Our public record is inspired by <a href="https://forrt.org/contributors/">FORRT’s Contributors page</a>, which connects people to specific projects and roles. PPBS adapts that transparent, project-level approach to research, mentoring, data stewardship, infrastructure, and publication.</p>
  <div class="process-notes">
    <div><strong>Prospective contribution record</strong><p>At project launch, the team records the lead, mentors, contributors, expected outputs, anticipated CRediT roles, provisional author order, access boundaries, and unresolved decisions.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Milestone review</strong><p>The record is revisited at Gate B, after the main analysis, at the first complete draft, and before submission or public release. Credit follows the work actually completed.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Transparent decisions</strong><p>The lead researcher and programme steward facilitate a final proposal grounded in the contribution record, discuss it with the team, document reasons and disagreements, and use an independent route if a conflict cannot be resolved.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="methods-toolkit credit-resources">
    <h3>Tools and evidence behind this approach</h3>
    <p><a href="https://tenzing.club/">Tenzing</a> is a free tool for collecting contributor information and producing CRediT statements. It documents the record; the team makes authorship decisions from the work performed and the responsibilities each author accepts.</p>
    <ul>
      <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.3233/ISU-210109">Kovacs et al. (2021), <em>Tenzing and the importance of tool development for research efficiency</em></a></li>
      <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0244611">Holcombe et al. (2020), <em>Documenting contributions to scholarly articles using CRediT and tenzing</em></a></li>
      <li><a href="https://credit.niso.org/contributor-roles-defined/">Definitions of all 14 CRediT roles</a></li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <p class="caveat"><strong>Ordinary authorship expectation:</strong> the selected researcher is expected to lead the project and be first author when their completed contribution supports that role. Substantive mentors and the Founding PI may qualify as authors through the work they actually perform. Every proposed author must review the manuscript, approve the final version, accept accountability, and explicitly confirm authorship; nobody can be listed without that confirmation.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="registered-plan">
  <div class="section-kicker">09 · Registered analysis plan</div>
  <h2>Make the remaining decisions before seeing the answers</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">Most projects will use data that already exist. A registered analysis plan records prior knowledge and data exposure, distinguishes discovery from confirmation, and time-stamps the consequential decisions that remain before the main analysis. Independent researchers who have never accessed the restricted data can therefore register a genuinely prospective plan. This approach follows guidance developed specifically for <a href="https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2625">preregistering secondary-data analyses</a> and is informed by the controlled-access collaboration model of the <a href="https://www.psych.auckland.ac.nz/en/about/new-zealand-attitudes-and-values-study.html">New Zealand Attitudes and Values Study (NZAVS)</a>.</p>
  <div class="checklist-shell">
    <div class="checklist-head"><span>PPBS registered analysis checklist</span><small>The approved plan will be versioned and time-stamped before the main confirmatory analysis.</small></div>
    <ol class="registered-checklist">
      <li><strong>Question and contribution</strong><span>What is being asked, why does it matter, and what claim could the design support?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Prior access statement</strong><span>What PPBS data, results, reports, code, or related analyses have the researchers already seen?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Samples and units</strong><span>Which national quota cross-sections, independent samples, or longitudinal recontacts are included—and why?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Variables and scoring</strong><span>Exact item wording, coding, scale construction, outcomes, predictors, covariates, moderators, and provenance.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Hypotheses and target quantities</strong><span>Numbered predictions linked to the quantity each analysis is intended to estimate.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Inclusion, exclusion, and missingness</strong><span>Rules at the participant, observation, item, and variable levels, including planned sensitivity analyses.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Analysis pipeline</strong><span>Preprocessing, models, contrasts, multiplicity control, diagnostics, contingencies, software, and decision rules.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Informative design</strong><span>Precision, power or sensitivity, smallest effect of interest where relevant, and limits imposed by the fixed PPBS sample.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Outcome-neutral quality checks</strong><span>Checks that determine whether the data can test the claim without depending on the direction or significance of the main result.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Interpretation table</strong><span>What conclusions follow from different estimate ranges and uncertainty—not merely from whether <em>p</em> crosses a threshold?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Reproducibility plan</strong><span>Project structure, version control, code review, computational environment, outputs, and what can be archived publicly.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Amendment procedure</strong><span>How unforeseen changes will be approved, dated, justified, and distinguished from the original plan.</span></li>
    </ol>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="inference">
  <div class="section-kicker">10 · Research quality</div>
  <h2>Rigorous evidence requires more than one statistical rule</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">PPBS welcomes quantitative, qualitative, computational, mixed-method, and multi-study questions when the available data and expertise can support them. Quality means aligning theory, measurement, design, analysis, computation, and interpretation—not privileging one inferential tradition. The programme draws on <a href="https://forrt.org/clusters/">FORRT’s open and reproducible research clusters</a> and aims toward the transparency, rigor, and reproducibility principles articulated by <a href="https://www.replicationresearch.org/constitution/">Replication Research (R²)</a>.</p>
  <div class="quality-lenses" aria-label="PPBS research quality lenses">
    <article><h3>Substantive reasoning</h3><p>Define the theoretical claim, plausible alternatives, political and cultural context, and what the project would add to cumulative knowledge.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Measurement</h3><p>Verify item wording, response options, construct validity, comparability across waves or groups, and the gap between a label and what the instrument can identify.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Design and inference</h3><p>Match claims to national quota cross-sections, independent confirmatory samples, or recontacts; specify target quantities, uncertainty, assumptions, and alternative explanations.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Analytic robustness</h3><p>Use diagnostics, sensitivity analyses, multiverse or specification work, equivalence tests, Bayesian models, qualitative triangulation, or other tools when they answer a named vulnerability—not as ritual.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Computational reproducibility</h3><p>Use scripted workflows, version control, stable environments, clear codebooks, code review, and traceable outputs so another researcher can understand and, where permitted, reproduce the analysis.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Transparent reporting</h3><p>Report all registered analyses, separate confirmation from exploration, document deviations, share what can responsibly be shared, and make limitations visible beside the claims.</p></article>
  </div>
  <div class="methods-toolkit">
    <h3>A methods toolkit for stronger evidence</h3>
    <ul>
      <li><a href="https://forrt.org/clusters/">FORRT Clusters</a> — a connected framework spanning pre-analysis planning, reproducible computation, FAIR materials, replication, meta-research, and academic structures.</li>
      <li><a href="https://www.replicationresearch.org/constitution/">R² principles</a> — transparent materials, data and code where responsible, scientific rigor, and an independent reproducibility check for results papers.</li>
      <li><a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14679221/homepage/forauthors.html">Political Psychology’s transparency and registration guidance</a> — materials, data, code, preregistration, and explicit handling of existing or secondary data.</li>
      <li><a href="https://aaroncaldwell.us/TOSTERpkg/">TOSTER</a> and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177">the equivalence-testing primer</a> — tools for questions about practically negligible effects.</li>
      <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267">Sample-size justification</a> — multiple ways to justify an informative design, including precision and sensitivity.</li>
      <li><a href="https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations">OSF registrations</a> — time-stamped plans and templates, including secondary-data analysis.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <aside class="citation-audit">
    <h3>Citations are part of the research design</h3>
    <p>At Gate B and completion review, teams audit whether foundational, contradictory, methodological, local, and structurally under-cited work has been searched for and represented fairly. The audit checks relevance and intellectual lineage as well as whose knowledge becomes visible. It is informed by FORRT’s <a href="https://forrt.org/citation-politics/">Citation Politics</a> project and its <a href="https://forrt.org/citation-politics/toolkit/">citation-practice self-audit tools</a>.</p>
  </aside>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="completion-review">
  <div class="section-kicker">11 · Completion review</div>
  <h2>Review the whole chain of evidence</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">Completion review asks whether the project is substantively sound, methodologically defensible, computationally reproducible, ethically responsible, and reported in full. The same standard applies across every direction and degree of statistical uncertainty.</p>
  <div class="completion-grid">
    <div><span>01</span><p><strong>Substance:</strong> Are the question, theory, alternatives, contribution, and political or cultural context represented fairly?</p></div>
    <div><span>02</span><p><strong>Measurement:</strong> Do the items, scoring, construct interpretations, and cross-sample comparisons support the claims?</p></div>
    <div><span>03</span><p><strong>Design:</strong> Do the sampling structure, time structure, and identification assumptions permit the stated inference?</p></div>
    <div><span>04</span><p><strong>Analysis:</strong> Were the registered analyses completed, deviations logged, diagnostics examined, and consequential choices stress-tested?</p></div>
    <div><span>05</span><p><strong>Reproducibility:</strong> Can an independent researcher trace data provenance, rerun permissible code, and understand the computational environment?</p></div>
    <div><span>06</span><p><strong>Reporting and citations:</strong> Are all results, uncertainty, limitations, exploratory work, contributor decisions, and relevant literatures reported transparently?</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="caveat">A project may require revision or may conclude that the planned test was not informative. Neither outcome is a failure of mentorship. The programme will maintain an amendment and project-status record without publicly shaming researchers who encounter legitimate barriers.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="governance">
  <div class="section-kicker">12 · Governance, outputs, and learning</div>
  <h2>Open where possible, protected where necessary, accountable throughout</h2>
  <div class="governance-grid">
    <article><h3>Participant protection</h3><p>Data access follows the applicable consent, ethics, privacy, confidentiality, and institutional requirements. Openness never overrides participant protection.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Project-specific access</h3><p>Researchers receive only the data needed for an approved project, through an appropriate secure route and under the required onboarding agreement.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Transparent provenance</h3><p>Every project documents source files, samples, waves, variable definitions, harmonization decisions, transformations, and code versions.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Durable research record</h3><p>Where permissible, outputs include the registered plan, amendment log, code, computational documentation, manuscript or report, accessible summary, and contribution record.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Accountable decisions</h3><p>Review, access, contribution, authorship, and completion decisions use stated criteria, documented reasons, conflicts-of-interest procedures, and a route for correction or appeal.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Evaluation and change</h3><p>PPBS will assess reach, fairness, mentoring quality, completion, reproducibility, researcher learning, and longer-term outcomes, then record what it changes for later cohorts.</p></article>
  </div>
  <div class="responsibility-check" aria-labelledby="responsibility-title">
    <div class="responsibility-check__intro">
      <p class="eyebrow">Social responsibility check</p>
      <h3 id="responsibility-title">Rigour includes context, power, and possible harm</h3>
      <p>Adapted from <a href="https://www.replicationresearch.org/constitution/">Replication Research (R²)</a>, every project prepares a short Social Impact & Responsibility statement. It asks where a claim may or may not generalise, who shaped the question, who benefits, who may be misrepresented or harmed, and what historical or political context the analysis needs.</p>
    </div>
    <ol>
      <li><span>01</span><strong>Declare</strong><p>The project team records positionality, affected groups, foreseeable misuse, contextual limits, and relevant conflicts.</p></li>
      <li><span>02</span><strong>Trigger</strong><p>Any applicant, mentor, reviewer, steward, or affected collaborator can request an independent responsibility review.</p></li>
      <li><span>03</span><strong>Discuss</strong><p>A reviewer outside the immediate decision chain can seek relevant expertise and propose revisions or safeguards.</p></li>
      <li><span>04</span><strong>Record</strong><p>The response, decision, dissent, and route for correction or appeal become part of the project record.</p></li>
    </ol>
    <p class="responsibility-check__safeguard"><strong>Safeguard:</strong> raising a concern in good faith must not reduce a participant’s access, credit, authorship consideration, or future opportunities. Independence, confidentiality, and anti-retaliation language must be confirmed before the pilot opens.</p>
  </div>
  <aside class="citation-audit" aria-labelledby="coi-guidance-title">
    <h3 id="coi-guidance-title">Conflicts of interest are declared, reviewed, and managed</h3>
    <p>Applicants, mentors, reviewers, data stewards, and decision-makers declare relevant financial and non-financial interests when they join a project and update the declaration when circumstances change. Relevant interests can include funding, employment or supervisory relationships, close personal relationships, rivalry, academic competition, prior public commitments, political or advocacy ties, intellectual investment in a claim, or a role in the work being evaluated. A disclosed interest can usually be managed through transparency, recusal, an additional independent reviewer, or independent adjudication. The programme will publish a plain-language declaration guide based on the <a href="https://icmje.acponline.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/author-responsibilities--conflicts-of-interest.html">ICMJE recommendations</a> and the evidence reviewed by <a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s12910-025-01221-5">Hart and Rodgers (2025)</a>.</p>
  </aside>
  <div class="learning-record">
    <div>
      <h3>Participant voice belongs in the public record</h3>
      <p>With explicit consent, PPBS plans a programme-experience and testimonials page inspired by <a href="https://forrt.org/testimonials/">FORRT’s Community Stories</a>. Participants may describe what helped, what was difficult, what they learned, and what should change. Choosing not to contribute—or offering critical feedback—will never affect access, credit, or future collaboration.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <h3>The protocol should remain a living document</h3>
      <p>The pilot will be versioned. Changes to selection, mentoring, access, authorship, review, or evaluation will be dated and explained so future cohorts can see what was learned rather than inheriting invisible rules.</p>
    </div>

<section class="mentorship-section timeline-section" id="timeline">
  <div class="section-kicker">13 · Illustrative project trajectory</div>
  <h2>A flexible pathway with agreed milestones</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">A twelve-month rhythm may suit some projects, while others need a different pace. Projects differ in complexity, and researchers differ in access to time, infrastructure, language support, care, safety, health, and institutional resources. PPBS will agree extensions and pauses with kindness, context, and humane accountability. This approach is informed by FORRT’s <a href="https://forrt.org/awop/">Academic Wheel of Privilege</a> and by the milestone-based, community-developed approach of its <a href="https://forrt.org/best-practices-psychology/">Best-Practice Guidelines for Psychology</a> project led by Lisa Spitzer.</p>
  <div class="timeline" role="list">
    <div role="listitem"><span>Start</span><strong>Orientation & compact</strong><small>Roles, needs, access boundaries, working rhythm</small></div>
    <div role="listitem"><span>Gate A</span><strong>Proposal development</strong><small>Mentor matching, evidence map, question and design revision</small></div>
    <div role="listitem"><span>Gate B</span><strong>Plan & access</strong><small>Registered plan, onboarding, reproducible setup</small></div>
    <div role="listitem"><span>Research cycle</span><strong>Analysis & interpretation</strong><small>Code review, peer meetings, documented amendments</small></div>
    <div role="listitem"><span>Quality checkpoint</span><strong>Completion review</strong><small>Evidence chain, citations, reproducibility, full reporting</small></div>
    <div role="listitem"><span>Outputs</span><strong>Release & reflection</strong><small>Paper or report, archive, public summary, learning record</small></div>
  </div>
  <p class="caveat"><strong>Equitable pacing:</strong> each project agrees a target rhythm and review points, then revises them when circumstances change. Reliable progress can take different forms and different amounts of time.</p>

<section class="mentorship-section faq-section" id="faq">
  <div class="section-kicker">14 · Programme questions</div>
  <h2>The short mentorship FAQ</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">Questions about PPBS history, data infrastructures, project families, access models, and the programme’s intellectual influences now live in the <a href="faq.html">full PPBS questions and answers</a>.</p>
  <details><summary>Are applications open?</summary><p>Not yet. PPBS will publish the final eligibility rules, review rubric, timetable, available datasets, mentor capacity, and application form before opening the pilot.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Does selection provide access to all PPBS data?</summary><p>Access is project-specific, staged, and governed by data availability, consent, ethics, confidentiality, institutional requirements, the approved plan, and the onboarding agreement. A project may begin with one bounded dataset or variable set, then add an independent replication sample, another wave, a country comparison, or longitudinal recontacts when the research plan and team capacity justify that step.</p></details>
  <details><summary>What can be registered when the data already exist?</summary><p>A researcher who has never accessed the restricted data can prospectively register the question, hypotheses, samples, variables, scoring, exclusions, models, contingencies, diagnostics, and interpretation rules before access. Researchers with prior exposure declare exactly what they have seen and register the consequential decisions that remain. Blinded or held-out samples can preserve a stronger confirmatory test.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Can I propose an exploratory project?</summary><p>Yes. Exploratory work can discover patterns, refine measures, expose boundary conditions, and generate sharper predictions. PPBS can then examine whether those patterns recur in an independent replication sample collected in the same year, across waves and historical periods, across countries or cultures, or longitudinally when recontact data are available. The strength of a claim follows the evidence accumulated across those designs.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Must I use a particular statistical method?</summary><p>No. The question and design determine the methods. PPBS often uses more than one method, model, operationalization, sample, or design so that a conclusion is tested against plausible alternatives. Frequentist, Bayesian, estimation, design-based, qualitative, computational, mixed-method, and other approaches are welcome when they strengthen the evidence.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Who will be an author?</summary><p>The selected researcher is ordinarily expected to lead and be first author when their completed contribution supports that role. Substantive mentoring can qualify for authorship; status or occasional advice alone cannot. All decisions follow the contribution record, CRediT roles, critical manuscript engagement, final approval, and accountability. Every author must explicitly confirm.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Will every contribution be visible?</summary><p>PPBS logs meaningful contributions using <a href="https://tenzing.club/">Tenzing</a> and the <a href="https://credit.niso.org/">CRediT taxonomy</a>, archives the record, and—with each contributor’s consent—connects people, projects, and roles in a public Contributors register inspired by <a href="https://forrt.org/contributors/">FORRT</a>. Work that fits a CRediT role receives named credit. Authorship is a separate decision tied to the contribution to a specific output and the responsibilities each author accepts.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Must a project finish within a fixed period?</summary><p>No. Teams agree a realistic trajectory and regular milestone reviews, then revise them as circumstances change. Project complexity, care responsibilities, health, safety, job transitions, language, infrastructure, and unequal access to protected research time all matter. PPBS aims to be a kind organization: expectations remain clear while pauses, extensions, and non-linear progress are handled with context and humane accountability.</p></details>
  <details><summary>What if the plan changes or the analysis is not informative?</summary><p>Changes are dated, justified, and distinguished from the original plan. A project can conclude that evidence is uncertain or a test was uninformative. Transparent learning is preferable to hiding a change or forcing a publishable-looking result.</p></details>
  <details><summary>How are conflicts of interest handled?</summary><p>Everyone in a review or project declares relevant financial and non-financial interests and updates the declaration when circumstances change. The review covers easily missed interests such as supervisory or personal relationships, rivalries, academic competition, prior public commitments, advocacy or political ties, and strong intellectual investment in a claim. PPBS records how each relevant interest is managed through disclosure, recusal, an additional reviewer, or independent adjudication.</p></details>
  <details><summary>How can a participant raise a concern?</summary><p>The pilot will name a confidential adviser outside the participant’s immediate mentoring and decision chain, alongside the programme contacts. A participant may request confidential guidance, correction, mediation, recusal, or independent review. The published protocol will explain who receives a concern, what confidentiality can be offered, how conflicts are handled, expected response times, and the route for appeal or escalation.</p></details>
</section>
