<section class="mentorship-intro" aria-labelledby="mentorship-intro-title">
  <div>
    <p class="eyebrow">Research access · sustained mentorship · applicant leadership</p>
    <h2 id="mentorship-intro-title">Good questions should not depend on privileged access.</h2>
    <p class="mentorship-lede">The PPBS Mentored Research Programme is being developed to support early-career researchers in producing rigorous, reproducible projects with Psychology of Political Behavior Studies data. Selected researchers will lead their own projects while receiving sustained theoretical, methodological, reproducibility, and data-specific support.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="principle-card">
    <img src="img/ppbs_head_logo.png" alt="PPBS tree-and-profile logo">
    <p><strong>The guiding principle</strong></p>
    <p>Selection opens a supported research pathway. It is not a prediction that a result will be statistically significant, novel, or publishable in a particular journal.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="purpose">
  <div class="section-kicker">01 · Purpose</div>
  <h2>What the programme is for</h2>
  <div class="two-column-copy">
    <p>PPBS is a cumulative research programme and research infrastructure. Its national quota cross-sections, independent confirmatory samples, and longitudinal recontacts create opportunities for theory comparison, measurement research, replication, and carefully qualified longitudinal analyses. Access to this infrastructure is currently proposal- and collaboration-based.</p>
    <p>The mentorship programme is designed to make that access developmental rather than merely selective. It pairs a promising research question with people who can help refine its theory, verify its fit with the available measures and samples, pre-specify an informative analysis, build a reproducible workflow, and communicate the results responsibly.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="promise-grid" aria-label="Programme commitments">
    <div><span>01</span><strong>Applicant leadership</strong><p>The selected researcher ordinarily remains the intellectual lead and intended first author.</p></div>
    <div><span>02</span><strong>Feedback for every eligible proposal</strong><p>Eligible applicants receive structured developmental feedback, not only a score or decision.</p></div>
    <div><span>03</span><strong>Method-neutral rigor</strong><p>No inferential framework receives automatic priority. Choices must follow the question, estimand, design, and data.</p></div>
    <div><span>04</span><strong>Outcome-independent evaluation</strong><p>Completion review evaluates adherence, transparency, quality, and interpretation—not whether results are exciting.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="pathway">
  <div class="section-kicker">02 · The pathway</div>
  <h2>From a short idea to a reproducible research output</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">The programme uses two developmental gates: first to select a small cohort for proposal development, and then to approve the registered analysis plan and project-specific data access.</p>
  <figure class="programme-flow" aria-labelledby="programme-flow-title">
    <figcaption id="programme-flow-title">Proposed PPBS review and mentorship process</figcaption>
    <ol>
      <li><span class="flow-number">1</span><strong>Short idea</strong><small>Question, contribution, PPBS fit, prior data exposure, learning goals</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">2</span><strong>Eligibility & data fit</strong><small>Measures, samples, governance, duplication, feasibility</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">3</span><strong>Structured review</strong><small>Calibrated rubric, narrative feedback, conflicts managed</small></li>
      <li class="flow-gate"><span class="flow-number">A</span><strong>Cohort decision</strong><small>Scientific promise, developmental value, equity, capacity</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">4</span><strong>Proposal sprint</strong><small>Mentoring triad, workshops, peer cohort, revision</small></li>
      <li class="flow-gate"><span class="flow-number">B</span><strong>Plan approval</strong><small>Registered analysis plan and appropriate data access</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">5</span><strong>Research cycle</strong><small>Reproducible analysis, transparent amendments, manuscript</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">6</span><strong>Completion review</strong><small>Adherence, quality checks, complete reporting, justified claims</small></li>
      <li><span class="flow-number">7</span><strong>Durable outputs</strong><small>Registration, code, manuscript or report, accessible summary</small></li>
    </ol>
  </figure>
  <div class="process-notes">
    <div><strong>Gate A is developmental.</strong><p>It selects a feasible cohort that PPBS can support well. Reviewers should not reward fashionable topics, famous institutions, or overconfident claims.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Gate B protects confirmatory work.</strong><p>Where feasible, researchers develop and register the analysis before receiving outcome-rich data. Existing exposure must be declared rather than hidden.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Completion is not results-dependent.</strong><p>A well-conducted project remains valuable when estimates are uncertain, hypotheses are not supported, or effects are practically negligible.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="eligibility">
  <div class="section-kicker">03 · Applicants</div>
  <h2>Who the programme is designed to support</h2>
  <p>PPBS intends to prioritize early-career researchers—including doctoral researchers and scholars in the early years after the PhD—with flexibility for career interruptions and non-linear career paths. Particular priority will be given to applicants in low- and middle-income countries, researchers from underrepresented contexts, and scholars whose institutions provide limited access to large datasets, specialist methods support, or international publication networks.</p>
  <div class="eligibility-grid">
    <div><h3>A strong application needs</h3><ul><li>A clear and consequential research question</li><li>A plausible connection to PPBS constructs, samples, or design</li><li>An honest account of prior access to the data or results</li><li>A realistic scope for the programme period</li><li>Specific learning goals and support needs</li><li>Commitment to transparent and reproducible research</li></ul></div>
    <div><h3>It does not require</h3><ul><li>A fully written manuscript at the first stage</li><li>Advanced programming skills before the programme begins</li><li>A prestigious institution or established international network</li><li>A promise of a positive, significant, or novel result</li><li>A Bayesian analysis plan—or any single preferred framework</li><li>Prior publication in an English-language journal</li></ul></div>
  </div>
  <p class="caveat"><strong>Important:</strong> eligibility, data access, ethics or consent restrictions, confidentiality agreements, authorship, and publication approval are separate decisions. Selection into the programme does not automatically resolve all five.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="application">
  <div class="section-kicker">04 · Application</div>
  <h2>A low-burden first stage</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">The first application should be short enough that researchers without extensive grant-writing support can participate, while still giving PPBS enough information to assess fit and mentoring needs.</p>
  <div class="application-grid">
    <article><span>01</span><h3>Research question</h3><p>State the main question in plain language and explain why answering it would matter.</p></article>
    <article><span>02</span><h3>PPBS fit</h3><p>Name the constructs, comparison, sample type, wave, or design feature you believe is relevant. Exact variables can be refined later.</p></article>
    <article><span>03</span><h3>Contribution</h3><p>Explain whether the project tests, compares, replicates, extends, or challenges an existing claim.</p></article>
    <article><span>04</span><h3>Prior knowledge</h3><p>Disclose any previous access to PPBS data, reports, code, descriptive statistics, or closely related results.</p></article>
    <article><span>05</span><h3>Learning goals</h3><p>Describe the theoretical, methodological, computational, writing, or career support that would make the project developmental.</p></article>
    <article><span>06</span><h3>Practical scope</h3><p>Provide the expected time commitment, relevant skills, constraints, collaborators, and target completion period.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="review">
  <div class="section-kicker">05 · Review</div>
  <h2>A review process designed for fairness and useful feedback</h2>
  <div class="review-layout">
    <div>
      <h3>Eligibility and feasibility screen</h3>
      <p>PPBS first checks whether the question can be addressed with the documented data, whether the project conflicts with an active or reserved analysis, and whether the proposed use is consistent with consent, privacy, and access conditions. This screen is not a scientific ranking.</p>
      <h3>Structured scientific review</h3>
      <p>Eligible ideas are reviewed against the same published criteria. Review combines scores with concise reasons, uncertainty, and suggestions. Scores are calibrated before use and should not be treated as mechanically precise.</p>
      <h3>Cohort construction</h3>
      <p>The final decision considers proposal quality alongside developmental benefit, representation, portfolio balance, mentor fit, and the number of projects PPBS can responsibly support. These considerations are reported transparently rather than hidden inside a single score.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="rubric-card">
      <h3>Proposed review dimensions</h3>
      <ol>
        <li><strong>Importance:</strong> Is the question worth answering?</li>
        <li><strong>Data fit:</strong> Can PPBS measure what the proposal claims?</li>
        <li><strong>Inferential clarity:</strong> Are the estimand and intended claims coherent?</li>
        <li><strong>Feasibility:</strong> Can the work be completed with available data and support?</li>
        <li><strong>Transparency:</strong> Can key decisions be specified and audited?</li>
        <li><strong>Developmental value:</strong> Would mentorship materially expand the applicant’s capacity or access?</li>
        <li><strong>Responsible use:</strong> Are limitations, participant protections, and contextual risks taken seriously?</li>
      </ol>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="safeguard-strip">
    <span>Anonymous review where feasible</span><span>Declared conflicts of interest</span><span>At least two perspectives per shortlisted idea</span><span>Independent adjudication of close cases</span><span>Structured feedback to every eligible applicant</span><span>Correction and complaints route</span>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="mentoring">
  <div class="section-kicker">06 · Mentoring</div>
  <h2>A team around each lead researcher</h2>
  <div class="mentor-team" aria-label="Mentoring team roles">
    <article><span>Lead researcher</span><h3>Owns the question</h3><p>Directs the project, makes documented decisions, leads the analysis and writing, and communicates support needs.</p></article>
    <article><span>Substantive mentor</span><h3>Strengthens the theory</h3><p>Challenges the contribution, construct interpretation, contextual assumptions, and connection to the literature.</p></article>
    <article><span>Methods & reproducibility mentor</span><h3>Strengthens the design</h3><p>Supports estimands, analysis planning, sensitivity checks, code review, workflow design, and transparent inference.</p></article>
    <article><span>PPBS data steward</span><h3>Protects data meaning</h3><p>Verifies variable provenance, sample definitions, harmonization, permissible access, and limitations of the available data.</p></article>
  </div>
  <p>The programme will combine project meetings, cohort workshops, peer feedback, and asynchronous review. Each team will begin with a mentoring compact covering goals, roles, communication, response times, accessibility needs, authorship expectations, disagreement procedures, and boundaries. More than one named contact will be available if problems arise.</p>
  <p>Mentorship or data stewardship does not automatically confer authorship. Authorship will follow documented scholarly contributions, discussed early and revisited using the CRediT taxonomy.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="contribution-credit">
  <div class="section-kicker">07 · Contribution, credit, and authorship</div>
  <h2>Make expectations visible before the work begins</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">PPBS separates access, work, intellectual contribution, and recognition. Data access, funding, seniority, supervision, mentorship, or membership in PPBS does not by itself guarantee authorship.</p>
  <div class="promise-grid" aria-label="Contribution and recognition principles">
    <div><span>01</span><strong>Access and administration</strong><p>Permission to use data, funding, coordination, supervision, and institutional responsibility are documented but do not automatically establish authorship.</p></div>
    <div><span>02</span><strong>Practical contribution</strong><p>Data curation, software, translation, visualisation, project administration, validation, and related work are recorded accurately.</p></div>
    <div><span>03</span><strong>Intellectual contribution</strong><p>Substantive responsibility for the question, design, interpretation, argument, or written output is identified rather than inferred from job title.</p></div>
    <div><span>04</span><strong>Recognition</strong><p>Actual contributions may be recognised through authorship, named contributorship, group credit, acknowledgement, mentoring credit, citation, or compensation.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="process-notes">
    <div><strong>Prospective contribution record</strong><p>At project launch, the team records the lead, mentors, contributors, expected outputs, anticipated CRediT roles, provisional author order, access boundaries, and unresolved decisions.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Milestone review</strong><p>The record is revisited when the analysis plan is approved, after the main analysis, at the first complete draft, and before submission or public release. Credit follows the work actually completed.</p></div>
    <div><strong>Authorship threshold</strong><p>Authors must make a substantial scholarly contribution, help draft or critically revise the work, approve the final version, and accept public accountability for their contribution.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="methods-toolkit">
    <h3>The full CRediT vocabulary</h3>
    <p>Projects use all 14 standard roles where applicable: conceptualization; methodology; software; validation; formal analysis; investigation; resources; data curation; writing—original draft; writing—review and editing; visualization; supervision; project administration; and funding acquisition. CRediT describes who did what; it does not, by itself, determine authorship or author order.</p>
  </div>
  <p class="caveat"><strong>Leadership and mentoring:</strong> the selected researcher is ordinarily expected to lead the project and be first author when their completed contribution supports that role. Mentorship alone is recognised as mentoring or in the acknowledgements; a mentor becomes an author only if their actual scholarly contribution meets the same authorship threshold.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="registered-plan">
  <div class="section-kicker">08 · Registered analysis plan</div>
  <h2>Prospective discipline for existing data</h2>
  <p class="section-intro">Most PPBS mentorship projects will analyze data that already exist. The goal is therefore not to pretend the data are new, but to make prior knowledge visible and protect the decisions that can still be made prospectively.</p>
  <div class="checklist-shell">
    <div class="checklist-head"><span>PPBS registered analysis checklist</span><small>The approved plan will be versioned and time-stamped before the main confirmatory analysis.</small></div>
    <ol class="registered-checklist">
      <li><strong>Question and contribution</strong><span>What is being asked, why does it matter, and what claim could the design support?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Prior access statement</strong><span>What PPBS data, results, reports, code, or related analyses have the researchers already seen?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Samples and units</strong><span>Which national quota cross-sections, independent samples, or longitudinal recontacts are included—and why?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Variables and scoring</strong><span>Exact item wording, coding, scale construction, outcomes, predictors, covariates, moderators, and provenance.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Hypotheses and estimands</strong><span>Numbered predictions linked to the quantity each analysis is intended to estimate.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Inclusion, exclusion, and missingness</strong><span>Rules at the participant, observation, item, and variable levels, including planned sensitivity analyses.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Analysis pipeline</strong><span>Preprocessing, models, contrasts, multiplicity control, diagnostics, contingencies, software, and decision rules.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Informative design</strong><span>Precision, power or sensitivity, smallest effect of interest where relevant, and limits imposed by the fixed PPBS sample.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Outcome-neutral quality checks</strong><span>Checks that determine whether the data can test the claim without depending on the direction or significance of the main result.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Interpretation table</strong><span>What conclusions follow from different estimate ranges and uncertainty—not merely from whether <em>p</em> crosses a threshold?</span></li>
      <li><strong>Reproducibility plan</strong><span>Project structure, version control, code review, computational environment, outputs, and what can be archived publicly.</span></li>
      <li><strong>Amendment procedure</strong><span>How unforeseen changes will be approved, dated, justified, and distinguished from the original plan.</span></li>
    </ol>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="inference">
  <div class="section-kicker">09 · Statistical and inferential standards</div>
  <h2>No preferred school—only justified choices</h2>
  <p>PPBS does not prioritize Bayesian statistics, null-hypothesis significance testing, or any other framework by default. Researchers should choose tools that match the question, estimand, design, and intended claim. Every confirmatory claim must specify what evidence would count for it, against it, or as inconclusive.</p>
  <div class="inference-grid">
    <article><h3>Estimate first</h3><p>Report effect estimates and uncertainty in interpretable units. Define the target quantity and distinguish statistical uncertainty from measurement, sampling, and design limitations.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Plan for the fixed sample</h3><p>Because many PPBS projects use existing data, justify what the available sample can estimate precisely, detect with adequate power, or rule out through sensitivity analysis.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Make “no meaningful effect” testable</h3><p>A non-significant result is not evidence of practical equivalence. When the hypothesis concerns a negligibly small effect, define defensible equivalence bounds in advance and consider TOST, interval-based inference, or another suitable method.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Control researcher degrees of freedom</h3><p>Map each hypothesis to its analysis, pre-specify contingencies as if–then decisions, address multiplicity, and separate confirmatory analyses from robustness and exploration.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Use robustness with purpose</h3><p>Robustness checks should probe named assumptions or analytic decisions. They should not become an unstructured search for a preferred result.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Respect the design</h3><p>National quota samples are not probability samples, repeated cross-sections are not panels, and between-person associations are not within-person effects. Claims must follow the actual sampling and measurement design.</p></article>
  </div>
  <div class="methods-toolkit">
    <h3>Methods toolkit</h3>
    <p>These are optional technical resources, not mandatory methods:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><a href="https://aaroncaldwell.us/TOSTERpkg/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">TOSTER</a> — R and jamovi tools for equivalence and minimal-effect testing.</li>
      <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Equivalence tests: a practical primer</a> — introduction to TOST for common psychological analyses.</li>
      <li><a href="https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Sample size justification</a> — multiple ways to justify an informative design, including precision and sensitivity.</li>
      <li><a href="https://help.osf.io/article/330-welcome-to-registrations" target="_blank" rel="noopener">OSF registration templates</a> — including a template for secondary-data analyses.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="completion-review">
  <div class="section-kicker">10 · Completion review</div>
  <h2>Review the research process—not the attractiveness of the findings</h2>
  <div class="completion-grid">
    <div><span>01</span><p>Did the analyses follow the approved plan, and are all deviations transparently documented?</p></div>
    <div><span>02</span><p>Did the data pass the pre-specified outcome-neutral quality checks needed to test the claims?</p></div>
    <div><span>03</span><p>Are all registered analyses reported, including inconvenient, uncertain, or null findings?</p></div>
    <div><span>04</span><p>Are exploratory and post-registration analyses clearly separated and appropriately caveated?</p></div>
    <div><span>05</span><p>Are the conclusions supported by the estimates, uncertainty, design, measurement, and sampling limitations?</p></div>
    <div><span>06</span><p>Are code, documentation, and shareable outputs sufficient for an independent researcher to understand and reproduce the workflow?</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="caveat">A project may require revision or may conclude that the planned test was not informative. Neither outcome is a failure of mentorship. The programme will maintain an amendment and project-status record without publicly shaming researchers who encounter legitimate barriers.</p>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section" id="governance">
  <div class="section-kicker">11 · Governance and outputs</div>
  <h2>Open where possible, protected where necessary</h2>
  <div class="governance-grid">
    <article><h3>Participant protection</h3><p>Data access follows the applicable consent, ethics, privacy, and institutional requirements. Public archiving obligations apply only to materials that can be shared responsibly.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Project-specific access</h3><p>Selected researchers receive only the data needed for an approved project, through the appropriate secure route and under a separate access agreement where required.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Transparent provenance</h3><p>Every project documents the PPBS source files, samples, waves, variable definitions, harmonization decisions, transformations, and code version used.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Durable public record</h3><p>Where permissible, outputs include a time-stamped plan, amendment log, analysis code, computational documentation, manuscript or report, and accessible summary.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Authorship clarity</h3><p>Roles are agreed early, reviewed throughout the project, and recorded using CRediT. Data access or mentorship alone does not establish authorship.</p></article>
    <article><h3>Evaluation and learning</h3><p>PPBS will assess reach, review fairness, mentoring quality, completion, reproducibility, outputs, and longer-term skills—then publish what it changes for later cohorts.</p></article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="mentorship-section faq-section" id="faq">
  <div class="section-kicker">13 · Questions</div>
  <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
  <details><summary>Are applications open?</summary><p>Not yet. PPBS will publish the final eligibility rules, review rubric, timetable, available datasets, mentor capacity, and application form before opening the pilot.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Does selection guarantee access to all PPBS data?</summary><p>No. Access is project-specific and remains subject to data availability, consent, ethics, confidentiality, institutional requirements, and the approved research plan.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Does selection guarantee a publication?</summary><p>No. PPBS can provide a supported pathway to a rigorous research output, but journal decisions remain independent. Programme evaluation will not depend on obtaining statistically significant results.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Must I use Bayesian statistics?</summary><p>No. The programme has no default inferential school. Frequentist, Bayesian, estimation, design-based, and other justified approaches may be appropriate. Review focuses on the fit between question, design, analysis, and claim.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Can I propose an exploratory project?</summary><p>Yes, if the exploratory aim is stated honestly and the analysis is designed accordingly. The programme will not relabel data-driven exploration as confirmatory research.</p></details>
  <details><summary>Who will be an author?</summary><p>The selected researcher is ordinarily expected to lead the work and be first author if they make the corresponding contributions. All roles are discussed early and documented using CRediT; mentoring alone does not guarantee authorship.</p></details>
</section>
